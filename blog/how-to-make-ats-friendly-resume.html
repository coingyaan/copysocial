How to Make an ATS Friendly Resume in 2026: Complete Step by Step Guide

📅 April 2026 ⏱ 10 min read ✍️ CopySocial

Introduction

If you have applied to multiple jobs and have not received any interview calls, your resume might not even be reaching a recruiter.

In the US, UK, Europe, India, UAE and across Africa and Asia, most companies use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to filter resumes before a human ever sees them. Research shows that over 70% of resumes are rejected at this stage.

That means even qualified candidates get ignored. Not because they lack skills, but because their resume is not optimised properly for how these systems work.

In this guide you will learn how to create an ATS friendly resume, improve your resume score and increase your chances of getting shortlisted for the roles you deserve.

What is an ATS Friendly Resume?

An ATS friendly resume is designed to pass automated screening systems used by recruiters and hiring managers worldwide.

These systems scan resumes for four main things:

  • Keywords that match the job description
  • Skills and qualifications relevant to the role
  • Job relevance and experience alignment
  • Clean formatting that the system can read correctly

If your resume does not match the job description or cannot be parsed properly by the software, it gets rejected automatically before any recruiter sees it.

Why Most Resumes Get Rejected

Before fixing your resume you need to understand what is going wrong. Most rejections come down to three core problems.

Missing Keywords

ATS systems look for exact keywords from the job description. If your resume does not include them your score drops immediately. A resume that reads beautifully to a human can score zero with an ATS if the right terms are absent.

Poor Formatting

Using tables, graphics, columns or fancy designs can completely break ATS parsing. The system cannot extract text from visual elements and simply skips them. Your experience section might be invisible to the software even if it is the strongest part of your resume.

Generic Content Without Results

Words like "hardworking" or "team player" do not add value unless backed by measurable results. ATS systems and recruiters both weight specific achievements over generic descriptions.

Step by Step Guide to Create an ATS Friendly Resume

Step 1
Use a Simple Resume Format

ATS systems prefer clean and structured resumes. Follow these formatting rules:

  • Use standard fonts like Arial or Calibri at 10 to 12pt
  • Avoid tables, icons, images and graphics entirely
  • Use bullet points for experience and achievements
  • Keep to a single column layout
  • Use consistent spacing and margins
Step 2
Add Job-Specific Keywords

This is the most important step. Carefully read the job description and include the exact keywords the employer uses.

Look for and include:

  • Required skills and tools
  • Technologies and software mentioned
  • Job titles and industry terms
  • Certifications or qualifications listed

For example, if a job requires Python, SQL and Data Analysis, make sure those exact terms appear in your resume. Do not paraphrase them.

Step 3
Optimise Your Resume Headline

Instead of writing "Looking for opportunities", write something specific and keyword-rich like:

Data Analyst | SQL | Python | 3 Years Experience

This improves both your ATS score and the impression you make when a recruiter does read your resume.

Step 4
Focus on Achievements Not Duties

Replace generic duty descriptions with specific achievements that include numbers.

Weak: Responsible for managing projects

Strong: Managed 5 concurrent projects and improved team efficiency by 30%

Always include numbers, percentages, timeframes and outcomes. Quantified achievements significantly boost your ATS score.

Step 5
Use Standard Section Headings

ATS systems look for specific section labels to categorise your content. Use these standard headings:

  • Work Experience
  • Skills
  • Education
  • Professional Summary
  • Certifications

Avoid creative headings like "My Journey" or "What I Have Done." The ATS will not recognise them and your content may be missed entirely.

Step 6
Save in the Right Format

The safest formats for ATS submission are:

  • PDF: widely accepted and preserves formatting
  • DOCX: preferred by some older ATS systems

Never submit a resume as an image, JPEG or scanned document. The ATS cannot read text from image files.

ATS Resume Structure (Recommended Order)

Structure your resume in this order for the best ATS performance:

  1. Name and contact information (email, phone, LinkedIn)
  2. Professional Summary (3 to 4 sentences with keywords)
  3. Skills (list format with relevant keywords)
  4. Work Experience (reverse chronological, with achievements)
  5. Education
  6. Certifications (if applicable)

US vs Europe vs India Resume Differences

United States

US resumes are typically 1 to 2 pages. Do not include personal details such as a photo, age or gender. Focus heavily on measurable achievements and quantified results. ATS usage is extremely high among US employers especially for corporate and tech roles.

United Kingdom and Europe

UK and European employers use the term CV rather than resume. CVs can be longer than US resumes. The Europass format is common across EU countries. Some countries allow personal information but check regional norms before including a photo.

India

Indian resumes often include a career objective and are typically 2 to 3 pages. Platforms like Naukri and LinkedIn are the primary sourcing tools. ATS usage is growing rapidly among Indian multinational employers and tech companies. Keep formatting clean and keyword-rich for best results.

UAE and Gulf

Gulf employers value a clear professional summary and relevant certifications. ATS adoption is increasing significantly across UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait. Keep formatting simple and include region-relevant keywords for your target industry.

Common ATS Resume Mistakes to Avoid

These mistakes will get your resume rejected automatically:

  • Using images, icons or graphics anywhere in the resume
  • Adding too many colours or decorative design elements
  • Not customising your resume for each job application
  • Keyword stuffing without context (ATS and recruiters both penalise this)
  • Using complex multi-column formatting
  • Submitting as a scanned PDF or image file
  • Using headers or footers for contact information (many ATS systems skip these)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an ATS friendly resume?

An ATS friendly resume is designed to be read and parsed correctly by Applicant Tracking Systems. It uses simple formatting, standard section headers, relevant keywords and no graphics or tables that confuse automated screening software.

What resume format is best for ATS?

PDF and DOCX are both accepted by most ATS systems. Use a clean single-column layout with standard fonts. Avoid tables, text boxes, columns and images. The content matters more than the design.

How do I know if my resume is ATS friendly?

Should I customise my resume for every job?

Yes, always. ATS systems score your resume against the specific job description. A resume that scores 80 for one role may score 55 for another because the keywords differ. Tailoring your resume for each application is one of the highest-impact things you can do.

How many keywords should I include?

Include all relevant keywords from the job description naturally in context. Do not stuff keywords in ways that make sentences unreadable. Quality and relevance matter more than quantity.
